Euler diagnostics

    DG/FV lab

    Euler flow over a body solves the two-dimensional inviscid compressible Euler equations with the same vector DG operator used by the scalar cases. Three obstacle shapes are available: a square (default — its walls coincide exactly with cell faces, so there is no geometry error and the entropy plot measures the scheme itself), a 10° half-angle wedge (for supersonic M∞ the θ–β–M oblique-shock angle is drawn as a dashed overlay, so the computed shock can be compared against theory; below the attachment Mach the shock detaches), and a cylinder (a stair-step approximation that illustrates geometry error on Cartesian meshes). Exterior boundaries impose Riemann-invariant (characteristic) far-field states, so subsonic as well as supersonic freestreams behave correctly.

    U=(ρ,ρu,ρv,E), F(U) and G(U) are Euler fluxes, and p=(γ−1)(E−ρ(u²+v²)/2). The face flux is local Lax–Friedrichs/Rusanov: F̂=½(F_L+F_R)−½α a_max(U_R−U_L).

    Nx, Ny are the mesh dimensions. CFL controls the explicit time step. spf is solver steps per animation frame. M∞ is freestream Mach number. R is cylinder radius. γ is the ratio of specific heats.

    All cases use modal Legendre coefficients. P0/Q0 is the finite-volume limit; higher degrees add modes inside each cell, and the Euler plot is sampled on a subcell grid so you can see the extra in-cell structure. The stab chip switches the Euler limiter live: off (raw DG — expect blow-up at shocks), positivity (Zhang–Shu scaling only), pos+minmod (troubled cells become limited linears), pos+flatten (troubled cells drop to their means).

    Things to try: compare P0 (FV) with P1/P2 at the same DOF count; run M∞=0.5 vs 1.5 and watch the bow shock appear; on the wedge, refine the mesh and watch the computed shock converge to the dashed theoretical angle, or drop M∞ toward ~1.4 to see the shock detach; compare entropy between the square (exact walls) and the cylinder (stair-step) at the same resolution; watch the Cd/Cl history settle (or oscillate) in the diagnostics panel; lower α or the limiter strength until the scheme fails.

    Euler is inviscid — drag here is wave/numerical drag, not a Navier–Stokes prediction.

    Hotkeys: Space run/pause, R reset, S one step, H help, [/] degree, 1 Euler, 2 diamond, 3 advection, 4 Burgers.
